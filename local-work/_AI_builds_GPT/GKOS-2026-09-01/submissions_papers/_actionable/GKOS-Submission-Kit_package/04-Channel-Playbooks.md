# Channel playbooks

Instructions checked 2026-09-08 unless marked reconfirm. Payload sizes below are editorial recommendations, not official limits. Recheck the live instructions immediately before sending. Keep recipient-native forms authoritative; the internal JSON manifest is not a substitute.

## NIST AI 300-1

Source: [official draft](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.300-1.ipd.pdf).

- Route: ai-standards+doczd@nist.gov, the specific documentation-feedback address.
- Timing: September 16, 2026 for the indicated revision; later input may still be considered for subsequent work. No precise cutoff timezone asserted here.
- Scope: public dataset/model documentation. Reviewer instructions appear on PDF page 7. The optional commenting template is encouraged; other forms are accepted. Disclose AI assistance.

1. Download the official document and optional template through the project page; record exact edition and hash.
2. Select two to four comments in scope. Suggested topics: revision-aware references, correction/supersession, evidence-status clarity and exchangeability. Inspect existing provisions first.
3. Use template 02 for each row; verify target line numbers. Keep actor authorization expiry out unless its relevance is specifically established; label whole-system ideas future work.
4. Recommended payload: brief covering note, editable comment template, optional compact evidence annex. Include source citations relevant to those comments; do not attach a priority dossier by default.
5. Complete human review and truthful AI disclosure. Suggested subject: Comments on NIST AI 300-1 ipd — Shaun Marshall — documentation traceability.
6. Send through the stated email route and retain native email, exact attachments and any response. Record public posting separately.

Success: a useful attributed contribution delivered; acknowledgment or incorporation is a later event. [NIST's project invitation](https://www.nist.gov/artificial-intelligence/nists-ai-standards-zero-drafts-pilot-project-accelerate-standardization) says significant contributions may be acknowledged, not that they will be.

## OECD.AI catalogue

Source: [actual tool form](https://oecd.ai/en/catalogue/tools/submit), [contribution page](https://oecd.ai/en/catalogue/contribute).

1. Start with a GKOS-Engine entry tied to a verified version. Check whether a separate standard/guideline entry is eligible and nonduplicative before making one.
2. Prepare name, excerpt (maximum 80 words in the form), detailed description, stable URL, creator attribution and supporting evidence. Supply other fields according to the form's actual labels and choices; do not invent taxonomy selections.
3. Describe implemented functions, intended users and limitations. Distinguish the implementation license from the standard's documentation license. Use verified DOI links only.
4. Identify yourself as creator where appropriate; do not invent an organization to satisfy a field. Ask the catalogue contact if a required field does not fit.
5. Follow actual authentication, terms and disclosure prompts. Save the completed field text before submitting.
6. Retain confirmation and check the eventual listing for attribution and accurate scope. No turnaround is promised here. The contribution page disclaims vetting/endorsement; do not advertise listing as validation.

Recommended detailed description structure: problem; users; implemented workflow; evidence emitted; integration requirements; limitations; version and licensing; creator and reference citation. An independent-use case should only be submitted once it actually exists.

## NIST AI 200-2 TEVV-Athlon

Sources: [call](https://www.nist.gov/artificial-intelligence/ai-research/tevv-athlon-framework-evaluating-ai-systems), [draft](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.200-2.ipd.pdf).

- Due October 6, 2026. Route TEVV-Athlon@nist.gov; include NIST AI 200-2 in subject. Use an unlocked permitted format such as PDF or Word.

1. Identify exact methodology clauses being improved.
2. Define one objective: for example, measure whether cited evidence remains reproducible and ineligible evidence is refused under declared conditions.
3. Map the example into the framework's stages, events, tools and measurement concepts; avoid proposing a full GKOS deployment as a prerequisite.
4. Provide a fixture table: ID, objective, input revision, expected result, observed result, status, evidence reference and limitation. Distinguish correct refusal from tool failure, skipped tests and unknown results.
5. Include altered source, unavailable source, valid source and ineligible source cases where supported. Authority tests require actual implementation and a stated time model.
6. Record the exact software/environment/configuration. Distinguish repeatable evidence assembly from deterministic model decisions. Do not generalize selected test success into profile qualification.
7. Recommended payload: comment table plus short assessment annex; link exact fixtures and results at an immutable revision.
8. Send, retain evidence, then record acknowledgment independently.

## NVD modernization RFI

Source: [Federal Register notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/12/2026-16371/request-for-information-rfi-on-modernizing-the-national-vulnerability-database-in-the-age-of).

- Regulations.gov docket NIST-2026-0100; document 2026-16371.
- Due October 13, 2026, 11:59 p.m. Eastern. The notice requires portal submission and rejects email, fax and postal comments. It asks for one submission, not duplicates.

1. Download the notice; map your comment rows to exact numbered questions and subquestions.
2. Choose issues with a concrete vulnerability-data application: provenance of enrichment, correction lineage, versioned sources or machine-consumable uncertainty.
3. Show one synthetic or public-data example and state which it is. Explain compatibility with existing consumers and operational burden.
4. Prepare a brief with question IDs and concrete recommendations, plus references. Include supporting material not widely published where the notice calls for it.
5. Open the official notice's comment link; verify docket/title and current attachment limits. Identify as an individual unless another status is accurate. FAC is not a default affiliation.
6. Review all public material; the notice says relevant timely comments will be posted without redaction.
7. Submit once and save the tracking receipt. Check status before retrying an uncertain submission.
8. Record the posted comment URL when available, and compare posted attachments to your originals.

## NIST SP 1353

Source: [publication page](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/1353/ipd).

- Route csf@nist.gov. Due October 15, 2026 at 11:59 PM; timezone not specified on the page, so submit early.
- Scope: guide and supplied prompts; not fictional organizational documents.

1. Download guide and actual prompt artifacts; record each revision/hash.
2. Identify prompt IDs and exact text to change. Focus on source correspondence, missing/conflicting evidence and review status.
3. Require the AI to report human disposition only if supplied by an actual record. Otherwise mark pending/not provided. Never instruct it to invent an approving human.
4. Proposed prompt idea for consideration: identify the supplied source and revision for each finding; distinguish observed evidence from inference; state missing evidence; preserve draft status pending documented human review.
5. Demonstrate the change using an illustrative case or properly documented execution. Label which it is.
6. Send comment table and concise evidence summary; suggested subject: Comments on NIST SP 1353 ipd — Shaun Marshall — evidence traceability.
7. Retain exact sent package and response.

## IETF individual Internet-Draft

Sources: [submission portal](https://datatracker.ietf.org/submit/), [instructions](https://datatracker.ietf.org/submit/tool-instructions/), [author guidance](https://ietf.github.io/id-guidelines/).

1. Decide whether the proposal solves an Internet interoperability problem. If the only goal is dating a document, use an archive instead.
2. Compare prior work and select minimum scope, preferably one exchange profile rather than all six record families.
3. Use an official RFCXML template; fill author/contact, abstract, problem, definitions, format, processing/verification, errors, examples, security, privacy, IANA and references. Use a proposed individual filename such as draft-marshall-gkos-evidence-records-00 only after availability checks.
4. xml2rfc renders RFCXML. If using Markdown, select a supported converter and its required syntax separately. Do not assume installing xml2rfc installs a Markdown toolchain. Use an isolated environment if installing local tooling.
5. Review Note Well, BCP 78 and applicable BCP 79 obligations. Do not replace required IETF boilerplate with GKOS license text.
6. Validate rendering and idnits. Get an exact-candidate review of behavior and prior-work characterization.
7. Check submission blackout notices; upload using Datatracker, inspect parsed metadata and complete any confirmation.
8. Retain version URL and announcement. An Internet-Draft is work in progress, not IETF endorsement. Archive persistence does not establish novelty.
9. Optional outreach: after author identities and scope are verified, invite technical feedback and interoperability comparison. Do not imply a promised citation or accuse copying from similarities.

## W3C Community Group

Source: [W3C process](https://www.w3.org/community/about/process/).

1. Check existing relevant groups before proposing a new one.
2. Create/use a W3C account; review participation and contribution agreements and any representation requirements.
3. For an existing group, introduce a scoped technical question and exact source reference, following its procedures. A contribution register alone is not a technical work plan.
4. For a new group, define scope, deliverables, related work, participation, decision process, maintenance and realistic chair workload; seek five individual supporters through the formal support mechanism.
5. Publish contributions and reports with accurate authors/editors and source references. Group formation and report publication are distinct milestones; neither makes a W3C Standard.

## CEN-CENELEC JTC 21

Sources: [secretariat site](https://jtc21.eu/), [official acceleration measures](https://www.cencenelec.eu/news-events/news/2025/brief-news/2025-10-23-ai-standardization/).

1. Ask the secretariat to confirm current project part/title, stage, responsible group, and route for a US-based contributor. Do not assume historical enquiry windows remain usable or WG4 is the destination.
2. Ask whether a national member/liaison sponsor is required and whether input belongs in maintenance or a current work item. Formal Vote is not guaranteed under the published acceleration measures.
3. Obtain lawful access to the exact draft and the requested comment template. Do not reproduce copyrighted standards wholesale in an annex.
4. Prepare two to three pages: problem, exact clause, proposed text, implementation example, evidence limits and related work. Keep mapping informative; do not claim legal conformity from a crosswalk.
5. Submit only via the confirmed route; request registration/reference and disposition. A secretariat acknowledgment is not evidence of circulation or acceptance.

## INCITS / ISO — restore this omitted channel

Sources: [membership](https://www.incits.org/participation/membership-info), [technical participation](https://www.incits.org/participation/technical-committees).

1. Ask INCITS/AI for current individual membership terms, fees, guest possibilities and contribution procedure.
2. Describe the narrow work item and provide a short evidence-backed summary. Ask how it relates to current documentation work.
3. Decide on paid membership using actual terms, not assumed prices. ISO contribution through a national route does not require first completing W3C or OASIS.
4. Follow the prescribed contribution template and document system; obtain an identifier and agenda/disposition record.
5. Preserve original attribution separately from later consensus editing. Participation does not mean the standard has been adopted.
