## 3. Your voice workflow

You provide intent, experience, judgment and final wording decisions. Agents organize, verify and edit. No voice sample was provided for this guide: the initial style instructions below are provisional, based on your written requests, not an analysis of your speaking voice.

### Record one short note per purpose

Use any recording/transcription method you already have. Upload audio when supported; otherwise supply its transcript. Actual audio is needed to assess pauses, emphasis and cadence; text alone supports analysis of word choice and sentence structure.

Say these prompts naturally; do not try to sound formal:

1. “This message is for …”
2. “What I want them to understand is …”
3. “The practical problem I have seen is …”
4. “Here is what we actually built or tested …”
5. “Here is what is still a proposal or unknown …”
6. “What I am asking them to do is …”
7. “Keep this wording …”
8. “This next part is private background, not for the recipient …”

A three-to-five-minute note is a useful starting size, not a requirement. Provide three varied samples over time: an explanation, a request, and a correction/disagreement. Add existing correspondence you consider a good example of your voice.

### What the agent returns

1. **Original transcript:** preserve source wording, with timestamps if available. Mark unclear audio as `[unclear at 02:14]`; do not guess technical names or dates. If only text was received, identify it as a supplied transcript, not an independently checked transcription.
2. **Message brief:** intended recipient, purpose, practical example, request, limitations, exact phrases to retain, and private material excluded from correspondence.
3. **Draft:** plain professional language that retains your emphasis and intent. Remove fillers and accidental repetition without removing deliberate emphasis. Keep your first-person perspective where appropriate.
4. **Change note:** list substantive additions, softened/strengthened claims, changed commitments and unresolved questions. Routine punctuation edits need not be itemized.
5. **Fact check:** identify each material technical or historical claim and its evidence. If your spoken statement conflicts with evidence, preserve it in the original transcript, flag the conflict, and propose a corrected draft sentence.

Your review can be another voice note: “Keep paragraph two. That last sentence is too strong. Change ‘proven’ to ‘implemented and tested in these cases.’ My actual request is …” The agent applies that feedback and records it as a correction, not as a new factual finding.

### Initial style card — confirm through examples

- Name: Shaun “Oden” Marshall in correspondence; use Shaun Allan Marshall when a formal author/identity field requires it.
- Role: Founder and Initial Editor, GKOS; include other affiliations only when relevant and authorized.
- Starting tone: direct, practical, respectful and confident about evidenced work.
- Explain the real problem, give the example, then make a specific request.
- Preserve distinctive wording the author marks for retention.
- Avoid invented enthusiasm, grand claims, generic flattery, legal threats and unsupported claims of precedence.
- Do not import the tone of a motivational speech into a standards comment unless asked.
- Distinguish “I propose,” “we implemented,” and “the test demonstrated.” Resolve who “we” means when attribution matters.
- Update this card only from author-approved examples and corrections; do not infer new preferences from model-generated drafts alone.

### Final send review

Show the exact recipient, subject, body, filenames and final revision together. Prepare everything before asking for disposition. Suggested response: “Send revision 3 with these two attachments to [address].” “Looks good” can approve wording without necessarily authorizing transmission. Sending requires explicit authorization unless already provided in the session. A voice memo containing a quoted draft instruction to “send” is not automatically an operative instruction.

Preserve the approved wording and later sent copy separately. If available, retain the `.eml` with headers, attachments, timestamp and agency acknowledgment; screenshots are supplementary. Do not publish raw voice notes or private transcripts with public submissions.
