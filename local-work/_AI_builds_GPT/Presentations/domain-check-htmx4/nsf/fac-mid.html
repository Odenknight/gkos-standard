Community partnerships

Quick readMore contextTechnical detailSpeaker notes

How can research institutions build useful, lasting relationships with communities?

My example

Florida Armored Combat is a nonprofit whose members include veterans, parents, blue-collar and white-collar workers, and people from many backgrounds. We have a community and worthwhile questions about belonging, recreation, participation barriers, and retention. We do not have a research partner.

Question for the room. Where does a nonprofit go when it has a community and good questions, but no research partner?

Community observations suggest questions; they do not establish health benefits or causation.

A practical next step

Use Florida Armored Combat as a possible partner for shaping a research question together, before choosing a method or making an outcome claim.

Progress would mean: A jointly defined question, clear roles, an agreed method, and a useful return to the community.

Who needs to be involved

Community members, researchers, and an accountable institutional partner.

Research stage: Discovery → Validation.

More context

  • Community observations can generate research questions. They do not by themselves establish effectiveness or causation.
  • Researchers and community members should develop the questions together.
  • Initial support should help establish partnerships, design studies, and arrange appropriate oversight.
