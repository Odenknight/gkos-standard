What the simulations actually showed

What can an impossible journey teach us?

Over five weeks a small research program ran roughly fifty numbered computer experiments on the mathematics of "warp bubble" spacetimes. This notebook explains, without physics training, what each experiment asked, what the numbers said, and what they did not say. Follow 25 next-step stories: what we might learn, why even a null result matters, and who beyond warp research might benefit. Open the technical notes whenever you want the equations’ side of the story.

Read this first. Nothing here is a machine, a measurement, or a demonstration. Every result is a property of equations run on ordinary computers. The geometries studied are known to require matter with negative energy, which nobody has. Where an experiment failed, the failure is reported as a failure. Historical figures are report transcriptions; the verification chapter records what was checked. Forecast percentages are subjective planning estimates, not measured probabilities.
